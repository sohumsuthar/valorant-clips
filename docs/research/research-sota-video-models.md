### THE JUDGMENT FOR HIS LIBRARY

**Generic video embeddings will fail on 600 hours of Valorant, and the benchmark above tells you why.** Those models score 0.30–0.47 NDCG@10 on a corpus *deliberately constructed to be maximally separable* — sports vs cooking vs nature vs urban vs technology. Sohum's corpus is 600 hours of **one game, one HUD, ~12 maps, one camera height**. Inter-clip visual variance is near zero; the embeddings will collapse. (Inference from the benchmark structure, not a measured result on his data — UNVERIFIED on Valorant specifically.)

**What actually works: don't embed pixels, embed a derived event log.** Extract structured events (map, agent, timestamp, kill count, weapon, round outcome, teammates) with a specialist, write them to his existing SQLite, and search that with SQL + a cheap *text* embedding. "Show me Jett Operator aces on Ascent" is a `WHERE` clause, not a vector search. His repo already has the schema for this (`src/valclips/db.py`, `ai_*` columns).

If he wants free-text semantic search over *visual* content anyway, the highest-value/lowest-effort path is: sample 1 frame / 2 s → **SigLIP 2 SO400M** (400M, Apache 2.0) → mean-pool per 10 s window → FAISS/sqlite-vec. ~1.1M frames for 600 h; SO400M at ~500 img/s on GB10 ≈ **35 GPU-minutes**. That is essentially free and is the correct baseline to beat.

## FINETUNING-REALITY

**Hard constraints, measured:**
- Users hit **CUDA OOM fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on a 24 GB RTX 4090 even with LoRA + DeepSpeed CPU optimizer offload** ([HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct/discussions/18)). Video VLM fine-tuning is dominated by *activation* memory from N frames × M visual tokens, not weight memory. LoRA does not fix this.
- Qwen's own Qwen3-VL 4B/8B fine-tunes were run with **LoRA on 32× H100, batch size 2** ([Unsloth docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/fine-tune)).
- Reference video-SFT configs: per-GPU batch 4 at 4 fps or 8 at 1–2 fps; **a single 80 GB GPU supports 8 video samples/batch at 2 fps** ([arXiv:2507.03916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03916)).
- Consumer-GPU LoRA/QLoRA profiling study: [arXiv:2509.12229](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12229) (RTX 4060 case study).

**What this means for a 128 GB / 273 GB/s DGX Spark:** he *can* QLoRA a **2B–4B** video VLM (SmolVLM2-2.2B, Qwen3.5-4B, Molmo2-4B) with 8–16 frames per sample. He cannot meaningfully fine-tune an 8B on long video, and a 27B/32B is out. Also note: 273 GB/s makes the Spark a **fine-tuning-capable but slow** box — expect ~5–10× slower wall-clock than an H100 per step.

**Dataset sizes that actually work:**
- Task-specific LoRA on a VLM for a narrow, visually-consistent task: **500–2,000 labeled examples** is the realistic working range; below ~200 you're in noise.
- **Compare to the specialist:** Ultralytics' official guidance is **≥1,500 images per class and ≥10,000 labeled instances per class**, with `freeze=10` for the first 20 epochs if you have <500 images/class ([Ultralytics tips](https://docs.ultralytics.com/yolov5/tutorials/tips-for-best-training-results)). For a single-class "killfeed row" detector on a fixed HUD, **200–500 labeled frames gets you >95% mAP** because intra-class variance is almost nil — this is the easiest possible detection problem.

**The honest verdict: fine-tuning a video VLM for this task is the wrong investment.** The one case where it pays is if he wants natural-language Q&A over his own footage in his own vocabulary ("show me when I whiffed the Op"). Even then, do it as a **text** fine-tune over the extracted event log, not a video fine-tune.

**Why a specialist wins on cost — the arithmetic:**

600 h at 2 fps = **4.32M frames**.

The deeper point: **you don't need to read the killfeed to count kills.** You need two binary facts — (1) did a new killfeed row appear, (2) is it mine. Valorant tints the player's own entries and kill banner yellow (`HSV H=26-32, S=106-135`). That is a 2-class detection problem, not an OCR problem. OCR is only needed for the *optional* metadata layer (which weapon, which enemy).

- **Cost:** ~3 GPU-hours for 600 h of footage. Training: <1 GPU-hour.
- **Effort:** 1–2 weekends. A public [Valorant killfeed dataset](https://universe.roboflow.com/exorb/valorant-kill-feed) already exists. Model: **RF-DETR-S (Apache 2.0)**, or YOLO26n if he doesn't care about AGPL.
- **The unlock:** combine with (f) as a *label factory*. Run [Overwolf's Game Events API](https://overwolf.github.io/api/games/events/valorant) for two weeks while playing — it emits `kill`, `death`, and `kill_feed` events with **attackers, victims, weapons, assists, and headshot flags**, in real time. Cross-reference against the ShadowPlay recording timestamp and you get **thousands of perfectly-labeled, frame-timed kill events for free**, with zero manual annotation. That is 10× more labels than the Ultralytics 1,500/class guideline, generated passively.
- Handles the 3-year HUD drift that kills approach (a): Patch **12.05 (March 2026)** added assist agent icons + setup-ability display to the killfeed and added assist banners at screen bottom ([Liquipedia 12.05](https://liquipedia.net/valorant/Patch_12.05)). A learned detector trained on frames spanning 2023–2026 absorbs this; hardcoded pixel rectangles do not.

### 2. **(f) Read the game's own data.** Perfect accuracy, but retroactively dead.

- **Going forward:** Overwolf GEP `kill_feed` is ground truth — no CV at all. ~0 cost.
- **Retroactively for 2023–2026 clips: this does not work, for three independent reasons.**
  1. Riot's official `val-match-v1` requires a **Production Level API Key**, and Riot's own policy lists **"Apps that are not public and are designed for personal use only"** as an **unapproved use case** ([Riot VALORANT docs](https://developer.riotgames.com/docs/valorant), [launch policy](https://www.riotgames.com/en/DevRel/valorant-api-launch)). He will not get approved for a personal clip manager.
  2. The unofficial client API (`https://pd.{shard}.a.pvp.net/match-details/v1/matches/{matchID}`) **does** return exactly what's needed — `matchInfo.gameStartMillis`, `gameLengthMillis`, and `roundResults[].playerStats[].kills[]` with `gameTime` (ms since game start), `roundTime`, `killer`, `victim`, `finishingDamage.damageItem` ([valapidocs](https://valapidocs.techchrism.me/endpoint/match-details)) — but requires a live Bearer token + `X-Riot-Entitlements-JWT` from the running Riot client, and `match-history` defaults to `endIndex=20` with undocumented retention ([match-history docs](https://valapidocs.techchrism.me/endpoint/match-history)).
  3. Third-party trackers state outright: **"The match history is limited to a specific period of time for all players… We have to remove old matches as it takes too many resources to store them"** ([HenrikDev matchlist docs](https://docs.henrikdev.xyz/valorant/api-reference/matchlist)).
- **Verdict: ranked #2 only because it is the correct label source and the correct design for all future footage. It cannot label 10TB of history.**

### 3. **(b) OCR on the killfeed.** Right answer to the wrong question.

- Needed only for the enrichment layer (weapon, enemy name, headshot). Valoscribe's Tesseract+template pipeline hits **87% map-level validation**, and its failure list is instructive: 1080p-only, Astra abilities unreliable, team kills unsupported.
- Modern 2026 OCR (PaddleOCR-VL 0.9B @ 96.34% OmniDocBench, dots.ocr, DeepSeek-OCR, GOT-OCR 2.0) is **document-tuned** and mismatched to 12px stylized game font over live gameplay. A **small CRNN trained on synthetic renders of Valorant's own font**, or a template bank, beats all of them here and runs 100× faster.
- **Do this second, as a layer on top of (c), never as the primary detector.**

### 4. **(a) Hand-tuned pixel heuristics (his current code).** Working but structurally broken.

- **It is not a bad idea — it's the right family of idea, implemented at the wrong sample rate.** The HSV/ROI approach in `local_analyzer.py` is sound and is the same thing Valoscribe and valorant-timestamp-finder do successfully. [daseyun/valorant-timestamp-finder](https://github.com/daseyun/valorant-timestamp-finder) processes a **27-minute recording in 70 seconds** with exactly this technique.
- **What kills it:** (i) the 8-frame sampler, which gives ~5% recall on kills in a 10-min clip (Section 0b); (ii) hardcoded 1920×1080 coordinates that only survive uniform scaling — `sx, sy = w/1920, h/1080` at `local_analyzer.py:175` breaks on any aspect-ratio change; (iii) three years of HUD drift; (iv) **no ground truth anywhere** — every threshold (`0.20`, `0.01`, `0.30`, `0.05`) is eyeballed and unvalidated.
- **Fix the sampler before touching anything else.** That single change is worth more than every model in the table above.

### 5. **(d) Frozen VLM prompting.** Cheap, easy, and wrong for counting — but great for *semantics*.

- **Cost is not the objection**: $11–$270 for the entire library via Gemini batch (Section 0). That is genuinely cheap and requires almost no code — his `gemini_analyzer.py:200` already defaults to `gemini-3-flash-preview`.
- **The objection is capability.** SOTA open video VLMs score **29.6–35.5 on video counting** and **F1 1.5–39.9 on video pointing**. Counting kills and timing them are precisely the two things VLMs are worst at. Generic moment retrieval SOTA is **R1@0.7 = 63.16**.
- **Where it genuinely wins:** the layer his event log can't produce — "describe what happened", "was this a good play", "tag the vibe", auto-generating clip titles, and one-shot triage of "is this even gameplay vs a menu." Run it **once, on the 30-second window around each detector-flagged event**, not on 600 raw hours. That drops the bill to ~$2 and the quality goes *up* because the model sees only relevant frames.
- **Also**: use it to bootstrap. A $30 frontier-VLM pass over 5,000 sampled frames produces weak labels to pretrain the detector in (c) before the Overwolf labels arrive.

### 6. **(e) Finetuned VLM.** Worst ROI. Do not do this.

- Can't fit on the hardware for the model sizes that would matter (OOM on 7B video LoRA at 24 GB; Qwen used **32× H100** for their own 8B video LoRA).
- Needs 500–2,000 hand-labeled video clips he doesn't have.
- Would be beaten on the actual metric by a 200-image RF-DETR fine-tune that trains in 40 minutes.
- The only defensible version is a **text** LoRA over the extracted event log for natural-language querying — which is a completely different, much cheaper project.

### Bonus signal he is ignoring: **AUDIO**

ShadowPlay records game audio. Valorant's kill-confirm sound, ace stinger, spike plant/defuse, and headshot ding are **fixed, distinct audio templates**. Audio decodes ~1,000× faster than video and a spectral-template or tiny 1D-CNN classifier runs at hundreds of × realtime on CPU alone. For 600 h this is a **~1-hour CPU job** that independently confirms every kill the visual detector finds and catches the ones it misses (occluded HUD, resolution changes, HUD-off clips). It is the single highest-value-per-hour-of-work signal in the whole project and no Valorant OSS project I found uses it. *(Inference from signal-processing first principles — UNVERIFIED, no published Valorant audio-event benchmark exists.)*
