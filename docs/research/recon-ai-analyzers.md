5. **Agent-specific UI.** The ability HUD probe is `np.std(gray) > 35` (`:210`) — a texture-energy proxy. 6. **Minimap size/zoom settings.** The probe is a fixed 30–280 box (`:180`). A reduced minimap leaves game world inside the box, lowering the `V<60` dark fraction below 0.3 and killing the signal.
7. **Killfeed name length.** The red-band row scan (`:233-246`) counts *vertical* red run-groups and is blind to horizontal extent, so a 3-character name and a 16-character name count identically — but it also cannot distinguish one wide entry from two stacked ones better than a 10-row gap allows. More fundamentally: Valorant's killfeed shows **all ten players' kills**, so "red bands" ≠ "your kills" under any interpretation.
8. **Streamer mode / overlays.** OBS overlays, webcam boxes, chat widgets, alert popups (frequently yellow/red) land inside the sampled regions and are indistinguishable from HUD.
9. **Crosshair color.** The crosshair probe thresholds on brightness `>200` (`:194`), so a dark-green or red crosshair on a bright background fails; a white crosshair on a white wall exceeds the 0.25 upper bound and also fails.
10. **JPEG quality / frame source.** Frames are `-q:v 2` JPEGs (`keyframes.py:46`). `np.std(gray) > 35` and Canny edge density are both compression-sensitive; changing `-q:v` changes verdicts.

- **Prompt is placed *after* the images in both vision analyzers** (`gemini_analyzer.py:98`, `claude_analyzer.py:98`). Since the images differ per clip, no stable cacheable prefix exists — prompt caching is architecturally impossible as written. The Batch API (50% discount) is not used either.

- **Temp-directory leak.** `keyframes.py:38` and `highlights.py:149` each `mkdtemp()` per clip. `cli.py:605-607` / `:644-646` unlink the JPEGs but **never `rmdir` the directory**. One leaked dir per clip, forever.
- **Silent timeout → plausible wrong answer.** `FFPROBE_TIMEOUT=30` (`config.py:73`). `_get_scene_score` uses `×3` = 90 s and on `TimeoutExpired` returns `(0.0, 0)` (`ffmpeg_analyzer.py:74-75`); `detect_scene_changes` uses `×2` = 60 s and returns `[]` (`highlights.py:69-70`). A timed-out clip is scored as "no action" rather than raising. My measured `_get_bitrate_variance` on a 90 s clip was **46.2 s on local NVMe** — a 120 s DVR recording or a congested SMB link will cross the cap and silently degrade.
- **The vision path starves itself of frames.** `get_clip_highlights(..., max_frames=8)` (`highlights.py:188-199`) calls `extract_highlight_frames` without passing `frames_per_segment`, so it stays at the default **3** (`:141`). The per-segment loop is `for i in range(frames_per_segment)` (`:160`), so **frames delivered = 3 × number_of_segments**, capped at `max_frames`. And `_merge_segments` (`:115-135`) merges any windows within 1 s, while `detect_activity_regions` steps a 5 s window by 2.5 s (`:96-105`) — adjacent windows always overlap, so contiguous activity collapses into one giant segment. Measured on my test clip: `get_clip_highlights(max_frames=8)` returned **1 segment, 3 frames**. The analyzer that could actually read a killfeed gets 3 frames out of 90 seconds, spread across a merged 90 s span — not concentrated on the action, contradicting the docstring at `:144-147`.

## WHAT-IS-MISSING

Verified absent from the entire repo by grep over `*.py` and `*.toml` (zero hits each): `tesseract`, `pytesseract`, `easyocr`, `paddle`, `torch`, `onnx`, `ultralytics`, `yolo`, `tensorflow`, `sklearn`, `scipy`, `faiss`, `chroma`, `lancedb`, `sqlite-vec`, `whisper`, `librosa`, `mediapipe`, `matchTemplate`, `SIFT`, `ORB`. Dependencies are exactly: click, rich, fastapi, uvicorn, jinja2, pydantic, aiofiles, python-multipart, httpx, opencv-python-headless, numpy (`pyproject.toml:10-22`).

1. **Killfeed OCR — the single largest gap.** Every kill event in Valorant is written as text: `<killer> [weapon icon] <victim>`. The repo reduces that to "how many red-ish horizontal bands are there," which cannot distinguish *your* kills from the other nine players', cannot identify the victim, cannot read the weapon, and cannot detect headshots or wallbangs. A crop + OCR of the killfeed band replaces `local_analyzer.py:225-247`, `_estimate_kills` (`:313-329`), and the entire ace heuristic (`:457`) with a near-exact count. There is no OCR of any kind anywhere in the repo. **This one change subsumes kills, aces, multi-kills, victims, and weapons.**

2. **Template matching for HUD elements.** `cv2.matchTemplate` is never called. Agent portraits, weapon icons, ability icons, and the spike-planted indicator are *fixed sprite assets* — template matching against a small icon library is far more robust than HSV ratios and gives you `player_agent` and `weapon` (both permanently NULL today for local analysis) plus a real alive/dead determination to replace the 0.20-brightness heuristic at `:93`.

3. **Agent / weapon classification.** `AnalysisResult.weapon` and `.player_agent` (`models.py:72-73`) and their DB columns (`db.py:92-93`) and their web filters (`db.py:317,320`) exist and are wired end-to-end — and are filled by exactly one backend (Gemini), which is rate-limited to 6 RPM. This is dead weight in the schema.

4. **Temporal modeling — completely absent.** Every analyzer collapses 8 (or 3) independent still frames into scalars: `max()` over frames (`:320`), `sum()` over frames (`:323`), `count >= 2` (`:353`), majority vote (`:376-379`). There is no notion of *when* anything happened, no event ordering, no round segmentation, and no sub-clip timestamps written to the DB. Consequences: an "ace" cannot be verified as 5 kills *within one round*; a "clutch" cannot be verified as the player actually surviving; and the `extractor.py` highlight trimmer has to fall back to scene-change density (`extractor.py:26`) because no detector produced a timestamp. Dense frame sampling (1–2 fps instead of 8 total) plus an event timeline is the structural fix.

5. **Audio — not touched at all.** Grep confirms zero audio handling; `-an` appears 14 times, i.e. audio is explicitly *discarded*. This is the cheapest high-signal channel available: kill-confirm ding, ace/"clutch" announcer voice lines, spike plant/defuse beeps, Operator/Vandal/Phantom shot signatures, and — because these are ShadowPlay recordings — the player's own voice/comms reaction, which is a strong proxy for "something notable happened." Audio decodes at a tiny fraction of video cost and could act as a first-stage filter to avoid the 10 TB full-video read entirely.

6. **Embeddings / semantic search — absent.** No vector store, no CLIP/SigLIP, no ANN index. With 10 TB and ~22 k clips, the useful product is "find me clips that look like X" or "cluster my Operator angles on Ascent mid" — none of which a 1–10 integer in `clips.ai_score` (`db.py:32`) can serve. The existing web UI can only filter on hardcoded enums (`db.py:305-325`).

7. **No detection confidence that means anything.** Four analyzers write four incompatible definitions into one `ai_confidence` REAL column (`db.py:97`), two of which are just the score restated. There is no way to threshold on "the CV was actually sure."

8. **No evaluation harness.** Zero tests touch `valclips.ai`. Nothing in the repo can answer "did my threshold change make it better?" Any serious version of this needs ~200 hand-labeled clips (kills, ace y/n, clutch y/n, map, agent) and a precision/recall script — that is a few hours of work and it is the prerequisite for every other item on this list.
