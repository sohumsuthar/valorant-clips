# SOLVED-OR-NOT-VERDICT

**No product solves this. Not one. If you point any of them at 10 TB of ShadowPlay files on a NAS, you get nothing usable.**

The specific failures, with numbers:

- **Eklipse** — hard-capped at **10 local VODs/month × 10 GB**. That is a 100 GB/month ceiling. 10 TB = **~100 months ≈ 8.3 years** of continuous premium subscription. Mathematically disqualified.
- **Medal** — the *only* mainstream product with a folder-watch path ("Sync an External Recorder"), and it still fails, because Medal's AI is a **capture-time** system reading the live game process (their own doc: Streamer Mode "obfuscates some information that Medal uses to detect in-game events"). Imported files land in a library as opaque blobs. Separately: unlimited cloud at $9.99/mo is real, but pushing 10 TB over a residential uplink at 50 Mbps up is **~18.5 days of saturated upload**, and the destination is a company whose ToS licenses your footage for AI training by default.
- **Insights.gg** — the only vendor that will even *sell* you 10 TB, at **2× Diamond = $458/mo = $5,496/yr**, and it has no documented bulk-import path; it records forward.
- **Omnic Forge** — real computer vision on gameplay video, but one-video-at-a-time upload/link, 5 matches/week free, no folder ingest.
- **UpForge** — the best 2026 AI coach, and it explicitly **refuses arbitrary local files**; it drives OBS to record *future* matches.
- **Allstar** — doesn't support Valorant at all.
- **NVIDIA** — never had Highlights for Valorant (Riot never integrated the SDK), and G-Assist is a settings/diagnostics assistant, not a video tool.
- **Riot replays** — your matches only, **deleted every patch**. Zero retroactive value for 2023–2026.

**The closest single product is [Valocut](https://valocut.com/)** — Valorant-specific, free, fully offline, accepts MP4/MOV/MKV, handles multi-hour multi-GB files, unlimited local scans, marks every kill on a timeline. **It is the one commercial thing that would actually run on his files.** But it's a Windows Store single-file clip extractor: no library, no database, no cross-clip query, no dedupe, no SMB/NAS traversal, no Mac, no batch. It solves "open this one 2-hour recording and find the kills." It does not solve "make 10 TB queryable."

# THE-GAP

**Every commercial product in this space is a capture-time funnel. The gap is retroactive, corpus-scale, offline, filesystem-native indexing.**

Five concrete things nobody sells:

1. **Retroactive analysis.** The entire industry's unit economics assume they were installed *before* you played, so detection is cheap live telemetry (game process, memory, event hooks) instead of expensive pixel analysis of cold files. Medal, Outplayed, Insights, UpForge, DOR are all forward-only. Eklipse and Omnic will do cold video but meter it to ~10/month and ~5/week respectively. Nobody's cost model survives 500 hours of cold decode.

2. **In-place indexing on network storage.** Every product assumes files on a local Windows drive or uploaded to their cloud. None traverses SMB/NFS and indexes without copying. For 10 TB, "upload it" is not a feature, it's a non-starter — and shipping it to Troveo requires literal physical media.

3. **Retention that matches an archive's lifetime.** Eklipse: 14-day free / 90-day paid storage. Outplayed: outplayed.tv deletes after 1 year. Riot: replays wiped every patch. Twelve Labs free tier: 90-day retention. **The products are built around a clip's 72-hour social half-life. He has a 3-year corpus.** Nobody's data model even has a place to put it.

4. **A stable, queryable corpus rather than a stream of exports.** No product gives you a durable clip ID, a schema, or cross-clip aggregation. There is no commercial way to ask "every Ascent B-main hold I've played as Chamber since 2023, sorted by outcome" or "my first-blood rate on defense by map by year." That query needs a database over the whole archive, which is exactly what none of them build and exactly what `valclips` already sketches.

5. **Longitudinal self-measurement without API backfill.** Riot's match API will not backfill 2023 matches and replays are patch-ephemeral, so the *only* surviving record of his 2023–2025 gameplay is the pixels. Any multi-year skill-trajectory analysis must be derived from video, which is precisely the expensive path every vendor avoids.

**Synthesis:** the industry sells *clip production*. He needs *archive retrieval and longitudinal analysis*. Those are different products, and the second one has no vendor because the addressable population is small and the compute is front-loaded — which is exactly why owning a DGX Spark (128 GB unified memory, one-time capex, zero per-hour cost) inverts the economics that make it unattractive to a SaaS.

# MARKET-DIRECTION-2026

**1. Auto-shortform clipping is finished as a business — it is a commodity with a $0 floor.**
Eklipse, Medal, Outplayed, Spikes, Choppity, Ssemble, OpusClip, Wayin, DOR, plus free OSS (Crispy, VideoHighlighter) and free Valocut all do it. The video-editing software market is $3.75B growing 42% CAGR for AI tools — but that growth is being captured by Adobe (35% share) and horizontal players, not gaming niche tools. The complaint in 2026 is never availability, it's **quality**: Eklipse's own Trustpilot/PH reviews say "4/10 hit rate," "tracks volume changes rather than actual clip-worthy moments," "95% of the clips they recommend are complete garbage." **Do not build here. Differentiation on detection quality alone is not durable — it's a model swap away.**

**2. AI coaching is real, consolidating, and going B2B.**
UpForge's architecture is the 2026 template: **auto-recorded video + verified Riot API timeline, fused**. Omnic is winning institutionally, not in consumer — exclusive NACE Official Performance Intelligence Partner (Jul 2026), M80, Stay Plugged In (Feb 2026). Mobalytics/Blitz/Aimlabs are API/telemetry incumbents at $5–10/mo. Consumer AI coaching is a crowded $9.99/mo knife fight; institutional (collegiate, team, org) is where revenue is. Neither serves an individual with an archive.

**3. The actual money in 2026 is gameplay video as world-model training data. This is the single most important fact in this report.**
- OpenAI offered **$500M** for Medal purely for its gameplay video corpus. Medal refused.
- Medal spun into **General Intuition**, raised a **$133.7M seed** (2025-10-16, Khosla + General Catalyst + Raine), on a dataset of **~2B videos/year from ~10M MAU across tens of thousands of games**.
- By **2026-06-18** General Intuition was in talks to raise **~$300M at ~$2B**, reportedly with Bezos and Schmidt.
- **Troveo** ingests **6,000 TB/month**, has paid **$20M+ to 7,000+ rights holders**, and now sells segmented gaming datasets: First-Person Gameplay (34.6K+ hrs), Third-Person (112K+ hrs), Open-World Navigation (61.2K+ hrs), Gameplay Demonstrations (167K+ hrs), Multiplayer Interaction (48.3K+ hrs), E-Sports Competition (6.2K+ hrs).
- **worldmodeldata** raised £7M targeting 1M hours by end-2026 (vs. a stated current largest database of 40K hours). **Origin Lab** raised $8M to license publisher-side game worlds.
- Market rate for licensed video: **$1–4/min**, deals $10–200/hr.

The critical spec detail: **every high-value gaming dataset Troveo sells requires "synchronized player inputs and microsecond action timestamps."** Raw video without input traces is the cheap tier. Video *is* the commodity; **video+actions is the asset**.

**4. Semantic video search is commoditizing horizontally, not vertically.**
Twelve Labs (Marengo 3.5 + Pegasus) sells it at **$2.50/hr indexing** with a self-managed deployment option; Frame.io and Adobe shipped semantic search into post-production; Akta shipped Gemini-powered archive search at CES 2026. The industry consensus quote is that "semantic understanding is becoming an expectation rather than a differentiator." **Nobody has applied this to a personal gaming archive** — but nobody has to invent it either, and a local VLM on a DGX Spark reproduces it at zero marginal cost.

**Durable value assessment — blunt:**
- Kill/ace/clutch detection from Valorant HUD pixels: **zero durable value.** Five open-source repos already do it, Valocut does it free, and a 2026 VLM does it zero-shot.
- Another shortform clipper: **zero durable value.**
- The *code* for an archive indexer: **low durable value** — it's a weekend of scaffolding for anyone with an agent.
- **The archive itself, if labeled and paired with action/telemetry data: this is the only thing here with durable value**, and its value is set by a market that just priced this data at $500M–$2B.

# MONETIZATION-HONEST-TAKE

**Straight answer: this is a portfolio and personal-tool project, not a business. Build it, but do not plan a company around it. There is, however, one specific asset-side move worth making immediately, and it is not the software.**

**Why it is not a business (four reasons, in order of how fatal they are):**

1. **The customer doesn't exist in volume.** "Person with a decade-scale personal gameplay archive on a NAS who wants it queryable" is a population of maybe hundreds to low thousands worldwide. Almost everyone else deletes; the whole industry's retention defaults (Eklipse 14/90 days, Outplayed 1 year, Riot per-patch) exist because normal users don't keep footage. You cannot build ARR on a persona whose defining trait is being an outlier.

2. **Every adjacent market has a free incumbent with distribution you cannot match.** Outplayed alone: 16.8M downloads. Medal: 4M+ MAU (10M MAU per General Intuition's own numbers). Blitz at $4.99, Mobalytics/Hakko/UpForge/Omnic at $9.99. Valocut is free forever and already does the local Valorant kill-detection core. Your price ceiling is ~$0 and your CAC is unpayable.

3. **Selling the archive itself is near-worthless as-is — do the math honestly.** 10 TB of ShadowPlay ≈ **245–815 hours** (at 1440p60/~100 Mbps → ~245 h; at 1080p60/~50 Mbps → ~490 h; at ~30 Mbps → ~815 h; call it **400–600 h** central estimate). At the headline $1–4/min that notionally reads $24K–$144K. **It won't clear anything close.** Three independent disqualifiers: (a) Troveo's first-person gaming spec demands **synchronized player inputs + microsecond action timestamps**, which ShadowPlay does not record — he has pixels only, the cheap tier; (b) their first-person bucket already holds **34.6K+ hours across 50+ games**, and labs explicitly pay for *diversity across games and genres* and *complete sessions with action/state pairing* — a single-title archive is the least scarce thing he could offer; (c) marketplace uploads require the licensor to **warrant they hold the rights**, and Riot owns Valorant's audiovisual IP — he can warrant his own inputs and commentary, not the game's rendered frames. Riot's creator policy permits ad-monetized gameplay video with added value; it does not grant him the right to sublicense Valorant footage as a training corpus. **Expected proceeds: approximately zero. Do not plan on this.**

4. **The technical moat is a model release away.** Every discriminating feature (kill detection, clutch detection, map ID) is reproducible zero-shot by a 2026 VLM, and Twelve Labs sells the general version at $2.50/hr.

**Where there is real, if small, value — ranked:**

1. **Portfolio/recruiting, and this is the strongest case by a wide margin.** The exact problem shape — *ingest raw gameplay video at scale, derive structured action/event labels, build a retrievable corpus* — is what General Intuition raised $133.7M and is raising ~$300M at ~$2B to do, what OpenAI bid $500M for, and what Troveo built a 6,000 TB/month pipeline for. A working local pipeline on a DGX Spark over 400–600 real hours, with a published benchmark and honest precision/recall numbers (note: **Crispy publishes no accuracy metrics at all** — beating "no published numbers" is a low bar and a real contribution), is a credible artifact aimed directly at the labs currently paying the most for this skill. That is worth more than any subscription revenue this could produce.

2. **Personal utility, which is genuinely high and legitimately unserved.** He is the customer, the gap is real, and nothing he can buy fills it. One-time cost of the buy-it alternative: ~$1,250 at Twelve Labs rates for 500 h (plus 90-day retention on the free tier and a per-query fee thereafter) versus $0 marginal on hardware he already owns. Build it for himself; that decision is clean.

3. **A small paid tool, if he wants it — one-time price, not SaaS.** The only defensible pitch against the incumbents is *local-first, no upload, no quota, no AI-training ToS* — which is a direct counter-position to Medal/General Intuition's default-on training clause, and that clause is a live, dated grievance in 2026. Ship it on GitHub + Microsoft Store, charge once. Valocut already owns the free end. Realistic outcome: a few thousand users, not a company. Ceiling is beer money and GitHub stars.

4. **The one asset move to make now, and it costs nothing:** **start capturing input traces alongside ShadowPlay going forward** — keystrokes/mouse deltas with microsecond timestamps, synced to the recording, plus match IDs from the Riot API while they're still retrievable. That is the exact schema Troveo, General Intuition and worldmodeldata buy, and it is the only version of "sell the archive" that has any future. It converts new footage from commodity pixels into the scarce tier. It will not retroactively rescue the 2023–2026 files, and Riot IP still constrains what he can warrant — but the marginal cost is one background process and the option value is the only real upside in this whole space.

**Bottom line:** Build it because the gap is real and no vendor will ever fill it for a market this small, and because the pipeline is the portfolio piece. Do not build it expecting revenue. Do not expect to sell the existing 10 TB. Do start logging inputs today.

- Eklipse's local-file path is hard-capped: premium-only, 10 offline VODs/month at 1 credit each, MP4 only, 10 GB max per file (https://eklipse.gg/help/how-to-import-streams-from-local-storage/) — a 100 GB/month ceiling, so 10 TB would take roughly 100 months.
- Eklipse Trustpilot as of 2026-09-13: 4.1/5 across 941 reviews, 71% five-star, 7% one-star; Product Hunt 4.6/5 across 58 reviews, with top critical reviews stating '95% of the clips they recommend are complete garbage' and a premium user reporting 5 of 8 uploads failed processing.
- Medal.tv has an 'External Recorders Import Locations' folder-sync that detects new files from ShadowPlay/OBS/ReLive (https://support.medal.tv/support/solutions/articles/48001210933), but its event detection is a capture-time system reading the live game process — Medal's own doc says Streamer Mode 'obfuscates some information that Medal uses to detect in-game events' — so imported files are not retroactively analyzed.
- Medal Premium is $9.99/mo or $7.99/mo annual; the June 2026 update moved clip uploads from a 30-minute cap to unlimited, added unlimited cloud storage and original-quality (4K) uploads.
- Medal is owned by / became General Intuition, a world-model AI lab; Medal's ToS grants an irrevocable royalty-free license to use UGC for 'developing, training and improving artificial intelligence,' with an opt-out at Settings > Medal AI > Research and Development (toggle relocated 2026-03-19; Medal's disclosure post 2026-03-31).
- OpenAI offered $500M to acquire Medal for its gameplay video corpus and was refused; General Intuition then raised a $133.7M seed on 2025-10-16 (Khosla Ventures, General Catalyst, Raine) and by 2026-06-18 was in talks to raise ~$300M at ~$2B valuation.
- General Intuition's dataset is ~2 billion videos per year from ~10 million monthly active users across tens of thousands of games.
- Allstar.gg does NOT support Valorant — its official help article lists CS2, Dota 2, League of Legends and Fortnite, with Valorant only 'coming soon' for Match History (https://help.allstar.gg/hc/en-us/articles/12025115940119-What-games-are-supported).
- Insights.gg 2026 pricing: Basic free 5 GB, Silver $39/mo 200 GB, Gold $79/mo 750 GB, Platinum $109/mo 1.5 TB, Diamond $229/mo 5 TB (~30% off annual) — storing 10 TB requires 2x Diamond = $458/mo = $5,496/yr (https://insights.gg/pricing).
- Insights Capture records forward automatically and does AI highlight detection plus per-kill/death/spike-plant timestamping for Valorant, but documents no import path for pre-existing local recordings.
- Omnic Forge pricing: Essential free (5 matches/week, 2 insights/match), Elite $9.99/mo or $99.99/yr, Forge Pro contact-sales; it does real computer-vision analysis of gameplay video via Twitch/YouTube link or manual per-video upload, with no folder or bulk ingest (https://forge.omnic.ai/pricing).
- Omnic.AI was named exclusive Official Performance Intelligence Partner of the National Association of Collegiate Esports in July 2026, and partnered with Stay Plugged In in February 2026 — its traction is institutional/B2B, not consumer.
- Blitz Premium is $4.99/mo and Blitz.gg reported 99.980% uptime as of 2026-09-01; it is overlay + API stats with no video pipeline.
- Mobalytics has no video or clip handling at all — it is Riot-API statistics (GPI hexagon, lineups, economy) with a free tier and roughly $7.99/mo premium.
- Aimlabs+ costs $10/mo, $9/mo on 3-month, or $7/mo on 6-month billing, and includes an AI Coach and Valorant Training Center integration — but it is a trainer with telemetry and never touches gameplay video.
- NVIDIA Highlights requires the game developer to integrate the Highlights SDK (Unreal 4.18+/Unity 5.6+); Riot has not shipped it for Valorant, which is why the archive is raw ShadowPlay DVR footage rather than auto-captured highlights. ShadowPlay was folded into the NVIDIA App in 2025.
- NVIDIA Project G-Assist in 2026 is a local RTX-resident assistant for hardware Q&A, perf diagnostics, game/laptop setting optimization and self-installing plugins (mod.io hub, Elgato Stream Deck) on RTX GPUs with 6 GB+ VRAM — it has no gameplay video analysis capability.
- Valorant's in-client Replay System launched on PC 2025-09-16 and console November 2025; replays cover only matches you played, exclude voice/text chat, exclude esports and customs, are not a normal video export, and are deleted every patch — so there is no retroactive coverage of 2023-2026.
- Valocut (https://valocut.com/) is the one commercial product that actually runs on an existing local archive: Windows/Microsoft Store, fully offline, accepts MP4/MOV/MKV, handles multi-hour multi-gigabyte files, unlimited local AI scans, kill-feed-tuned for Valorant, free forever for the core app with a Pro tier only on the roadmap.
- UpForge (Valorant-first AI coach, free tier 1 analysis, Plus from $9.99/mo for 5 analyses, Pro 15/mo) drives OBS to record FUTURE matches and fuses video with Riot's verified timeline; its web path accepts only clips pasted from Medal or insights.gg and explicitly does not support arbitrary local video files or YouTube VODs.
- Outplayed (Overwolf) is free with ads, has 16.8M downloads, supports 5,000+ games including Valorant, does live-only event detection, cannot record Valorant voice chat (it runs through the Riot client), auto-prunes local storage, and deletes outplayed.tv uploads after one year.
- Twelve Labs 2026 pricing is $2.50/hour for indexing, $4 per 1,000 search queries, $7.50/1M analyze output tokens, with a free tier of 600 minutes / 10 hours, 90-day retention and 100 videos per index; models are Marengo 3.5 and Pegasus, with self-managed deployment available via sales (https://www.twelvelabs.io/pricing).
- Troveo's gaming datasets are explicitly action-paired: First-Person Gameplay 34.6K+ hrs, Third-Person 112K+ hrs, Open-World Navigation 61.2K+ hrs, Gameplay Demonstrations 167K+ hrs, Multiplayer Interaction 48.3K+ hrs, E-Sports Competition 6.2K+ hrs — the first-person spec reads 'first-person video game footage paired with synchronized player inputs and microsecond action timestamps' (https://www.troveo.ai/datasets).
- Troveo has paid over $20M to 7,000+ rights holders, ingests roughly 6,000 TB of video per month (via MASV), accepts 1080p+ of any length published or unreleased with 1-2 week processing, and requires marketplace uploaders to clear all rights and sell exclusively.
- Reported market rates for licensed video AI training data in 2026 are $1-4 per minute ($1-2/min for commodity unused footage), with deals cited from $10/hr to $200/hr.
- worldmodeldata raised £7M aiming for a 1M-hour library by end of 2026, against a stated current largest database of ~40,000 hours; Origin Lab raised $8M to license publisher-side game worlds as AI training data.
- Open-source prior art already covers the core detection problem: Flowtter/crispy (156 stars, NN kill detection for Valorant/LoL/OW2/CS2/The Finals, default-UI-only, publishes no accuracy metrics), SphinxNumberNine/valoscribe (OCR event extraction from pro VODs), dhirajpatel22/valorant-aim-analyzer (EasyOCR kill-feed rows), SolvyrEryx/ValoVision (ViT killshot extraction), Aseiel/VideoHighlighter (local Ollama-based offline alternative to Twelve Labs).
- Neither 'Sharpr' nor 'Wisdom' is an AI coaching product: Sharpr is an esports iGaming newsletter acquired by The Insights Group, and Wisdom Gaming is a Bloomington MN esports production/marketing company with a Mall of America venue.
- The valorant-clips repo at ~/Documents/GitHub/valorant-clips is 3 commits, 4,464 Python LOC under src/, with five analyzer backends at ~/Documents/GitHub/valorant-clips/src/valclips/ai/ (local_analyzer.py 19,146 bytes, gemini_analyzer.py 12,633, ffmpeg_analyzer.py 7,763, claude_analyzer.py 5,961, stub.py 582), and its README states it indexes '2,800+ Valorant clips scattered across network shares.'
