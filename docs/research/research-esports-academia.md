## BOTTOM LINE

The single most important finding: **AMD published the exact paper for this problem in May 2025, and Valorant is one of its five games.** [Gameplay Highlights Generation, arXiv:2505.07721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07721) finetunes X-CLIP-B/16 on 7,153 annotated event instances and reports **95.0% accuracy on Valorant** (95.2% after INT8 quantization), 94.3% overall across CS:GO/PUBG/Valorant/Overwatch 2/Fortnite, at **0.15 s inference per 1-second clip** with a **192 MB INT8 ONNX model**. Their conclusion explicitly names the gap they left open: *"In our work we did not use the audio modality of gameplay videos"* and *"in CS:GO a unique sound is played whenever a kill event occurs."* That is the recipe plus the stated next step. It is reproducible on a DGX Spark. Their dataset is not public — Sohum's archive is the missing input.

Second most important finding: **there is no public Valorant gameplay-video dataset at any useful scale**, and the one large public FPS egocentric video dataset (Reka's CS2-10k, 10,000+ hours) was **taken down by Valve** (verified below). A 400–1,100 hour, self-owned, self-licensed Valorant archive is therefore a genuinely scarce asset, not a commodity.

## AUDIO-BASED-DETECTION

The audio case is strong and is the highest-leverage finding after the AMD paper.

**Accuracy — audio matches or beats video on FPS-style events:**
- **BattleSound**: 2D CNN on mel-spectrograms, **96.02% ± 0.08** weapon sound event detection on PUBG audio, at **0.5 s resolution, 16 kHz** — finer temporal granularity than any video method cited here. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9866824/
- **Enemy Spotted / BGG**: CNN-Transformer, **93.60% test accuracy / 0.9290 F1 across 37 gun classes** from in-game audio, plus direction and distance (0–600 m) recovered from stereo channel differences. Bi-LSTM (0.6068) and pure Transformer (0.5918) fail badly — **convolutional feature extraction over the spectrogram is load-bearing**; the paper's own conclusion: *"local temporal patterns in the spectrogram are necessary."* https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05917
- **Della Santa & Lalli 2025**: on football, **audio (0.8924 acc) outperformed video (0.8300 acc)** with fewer training chunks (~700 vs ~1,000). https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16100

**Cost — the quantified version of "audio is far cheaper":**
- **AVSlowFast** (FAIR) states it directly: *"the [Audio] pathway typically only requires 10% to 20% of the overall [computation]"*, and repeats *"its modest cost of only ≈10%−20% computation; by contrast, audio processing is lightweight."* https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08740
- That 10–20% figure is for a *joint* AV model. A standalone mel-spectrogram CNN (BattleSound-class, or PANNs CNN14) against X-CLIP-B/16 at 32 frames × 224² is a far larger ratio. The "1000×" framing is directionally right on **bytes** — a 160 kbps AAC track is roughly 0.3–0.8% of a 20–50 Mbps ShadowPlay mp4, i.e. **~30 GB of audio for the whole 10 TB archive** — but I found no paper that measures an audio-vs-video FLOP ratio for highlight detection specifically. Treat >100× as INFERENCE, not literature.

**The explicit open door:** AMD's conclusion in 2505.07721 — *"In our work we did not use the audio modality of gameplay videos... in CS:GO a unique sound is played whenever a kill event occurs"* — plus *"integrating an audio model"* as named future work. Valorant is denser in unique audio triggers than CS:GO: per-agent ultimate voicelines (with a pre-labelled public voiceline corpus, above), spike plant/defuse beeps, headshot ding, ability SFX, round-win stinger.

**Caveat from the literature:** Ringer & Nicolaou found their audio-only stream *"produced considerably more detected highlights than other modalities, likely due to the impact of in-game audio (e.g. gunfire) that had not been entirely removed"* — i.e. **audio-only over-triggers**. In their streamer setting that was contamination; for Sohum's use case (find gunfights) that same gunfire *is* the signal. The correct read: **audio for cheap high-recall candidate generation, video model for precision re-ranking** — exactly the cascade architecture Yahoo used in 2016 to get 18 FPS on one CPU (https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08780).

**Not applicable:** the entire crowd-sourced-chat branch — Autohighlight for LoL (https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/186731/1/Highlight_Journal_MLwA_2_.pdf), chat-spike detection (avg precision up to 83.18%), video–audio–chat fusion — requires a Twitch chat stream. Private ShadowPlay DVR files have none. Discard it.

## SKILL-ANALYSIS

**The honest state of the art: skill is measurable from *input telemetry*, and largely NOT from video.**

- **Crosshair placement and flick kinematics are well-formalized but need mouse traces, not pixels.** The Flicking Skill Assessment (Donovan et al., 2022) extracts **SPAR** (Speed, Precision, Accuracy, Reaction time) plus "swipiness" from **120 Hz mouse trajectories** by sigmoid fitting, across 32 pro/semi-pro Valorant/PUBG/R6S players. Its core methodological finding is that **a single metric cannot define skill — you must fit the whole speed-accuracy tradeoff curve**, and the curve intersection gives a strategy-invariant motor-acuity scalar. Data proprietary. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9744923/
- **Improvement over time is small, slow, and measurable only at scale.** Listman et al. 2021, over **62,170 game-days / 682,564 Gridshot runs**: hit rate moved **86.10% → 89.60% across 60 days (~0.03%/day)** while hits-per-second moved 2.72 → 3.78. Day-to-day retention 40–60%. **90% of the benefit comes from 30 min/day.** Implication for a personal archive: a per-session aim metric will be dominated by noise; only aggregated multi-month trends carry signal. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8720934/
- **Rank prediction from video, evaluated honestly, fails.** Zhang & Wang 2023 (arXiv:2307.12236), 1,620 CS:GO Twitch videos / 268 users / 4 rank sections, class distribution 56/19/13/12% so majority-vote baseline = **F1 0.500**:

  Authors' verbatim verdict: *"While the best single view model did outperform the lipreading baseline, it did not outperform the majority class baseline, and the full multiview model did worse than both."* And: *"we suspect that our model is picking up user identity information."* The 0.850 number is an artifact of user leakage. Genuine cross-player skill-from-video is **at or below chance**.
- **Predicting *outcomes* works far better than predicting *skill*.** The Valorant round-outcome paper hits **~81% from mid-round onward** using TimeSformer over minimap-derived tactical features — but note it needed **explicit tactical event labels (positions, events), not raw minimap pixels**, to beat its baseline. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17199
- **Multimodal Twitch rank estimation** (Belova, He, Zhong 2019): 425 ranked + 9,928 unranked CS:GO streamers, hierarchical Bayesian pooling over video/audio/chat, validated only by *correlating* latent skill with later ranks — no accuracy/AUC reported. https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01615
- **Applied prior art**: an OSS Valorant aim analyzer using EasyOCR on the kill feed with fuzzy matching and cross-frame dedup (https://github.com/dhirajpatel22/valorant-aim-analyzer), and **Valoscribe**, which extracts kills/abilities/rounds/health/armor from broadcasts — and whose authors report that **Valorant's custom font defeats OCR models, and OCR at 4 FPS is too slow for near-realtime** (https://github.com/SphinxNumberNine/valoscribe). This is direct evidence against the OCR-first approach and for the AMD paper's position that YOLO+OCR requires *"extensive per game engineering."*

## SCALABLE-LABELING

How the literature actually labels large video corpora without manual annotation, ranked by demonstrated payoff:

1. **Pair raw footage against already-edited highlight reels.** Cheapest and most proven.
   - SVHighlights builds **640.18 h / 320 two-hour videos** of labels purely by aligning full games to official highlight reels — *"scalable and cost-effective label generation without conventional per-clip saliency annotation."* https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06926
   - Honor of Kings uses **pairwise ranking constraints between professionally-edited clips and long VODs**, on the premise that edited clips contain more highlights. https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06189
   - Sun et al. 2016 ("Ranking highlights in personal videos by analyzing edited videos," IEEE TIP) is the ancestor of this idea, cited by Yahoo.
   - **Directly applicable**: any clip Sohum ever manually saved, trimmed, or posted is a positive label; the surrounding raw DVR is negative.

2. **Iterative model-in-the-loop annotation (correct predictions, don't label from scratch).** Yahoo's two-stage scheme labeled **300 h with one expert** by annotating ~10 h from scratch, training a CNN, predicting the next ~20 h, correcting the predictions, retraining, predicting ~30 h, and so on — *"about four rounds"* per game. Then highlight labels came from crowdsourcing with 4 annotators/video (min 3, max 7), Cronbach's α = 0.7627, keeping the best-agreeing 3. https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08780

3. **Parse structured side-channel data into labels.** SoccerNet auto-parses **6,637 annotations from online match reports** at 1-min resolution, then refines to 1 s. https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04527 — Valorant analogue: the Riot VALORANT match API. Caveat: the Riot API gates production keys per-application and, since 2025-03-13, prohibits enemy-ultimate tracking in third-party apps. https://www.riotgames.com/en/DevRel/valorant-api-launch

4. **Fully unsupervised pseudo-labels from recurrence.** Islam et al.: K-means pseudo-categories over concatenated A/V features → cosine-similarity pseudo-highlights → train. **68.30 mAP on YouTube Highlights with zero labels, vs 70.18 for the same architecture trained supervised** — a 1.88 mAP gap. This is the strongest published evidence that a no-label pipeline is not a big compromise. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13933

5. **VLM-as-labeler → distill into a small student.**
   - **LEViL** (2026): InternVL2-8B captions → 2,391-term verb/noun pseudo-label vocabulary → frozen InternVideo2-CLIP-S teacher soft targets → 3D ResNet-18 student from scratch. **54.3% UCF101 at 1% labels; 73.3% at 10%** vs 87.8% fully supervised. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21358
   - Training-free CLIP-based pseudo-label generation for weakly-supervised video anomaly detection. https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11015429/
   - Gemini 2.5 Pro with CoT+Reflection generating pseudo-labels **plus reasoning text** as weak supervision. https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4168/paper_9.pdf
   - Online In-Context Distillation: small VLM + strong teacher at inference via sparse demonstrations, no finetuning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18117
   - **Reality check on VLM labeling quality for esports**: on EgoEsportsQA (CS2/Valorant/OW2), the best model is **GPT-5 at 71.58%**; every open 8B model is **33–50%**. An 8B open VLM is not a usable labeler for Valorant events. A frontier model is — at frontier prices.

6. **Semantic dedup before you label anything.** NVIDIA NeMo Curator's `SemanticDeduplicationWorkflow` (K-means over clip embeddings → pairwise similarity → duplicate removal, https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/curator/curate-video/process-data/dedup) and HAVANA's hierarchical SNE report **>10× annotation-effort reduction** vs linear annotation (https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10641). For an archive spanning 2023–2026 with enormous redundancy (same maps, same agents, same angles), this is the first step, not an optimization.

7. **Active learning** — uncertainty + diversity batch selection, with keyframe-only labeling instead of whole-video review. Standard, well-covered, lowest marginal value here given (1) and (4).

**Scale grounding (INFERENCE, flagged):** 10 TB of ShadowPlay mp4 at 20–50 Mbps ≈ **400–1,100 hours** of footage (at 4K60 ~130 Mbps it would be ~170 h). Verify with `ffprobe` bitrate over a sample. At AMD's measured **0.15 s per 1 s clip**, a full sweep of 400 h ≈ **60 GPU-hours**; 1,100 h ≈ **165 GPU-hours**. That is days, not months, on a GB10 — the video-first approach is *not* actually compute-prohibitive at this scale, which weakens the usual "audio-only or bust" argument. Audio-first still wins on I/O: reading ~30 GB of demuxed audio beats reading 10 TB over Samba by ~300×, and **the network/disk read, not the GPU, is the real bottleneck on a GVFS-mounted share.**

## PERSONAL-ARCHIVE-PRIOR-ART

- **Ego4D** is the canonical lesson: **3,670 h narrated**, but **per-benchmark annotation covers only 48–1,000 h**. Even Meta, with 931 wearers and 74 locations, densely annotated <30% of what it collected. Episodic Memory = ~74K queries over 800 h. Models still trail human upper bounds badly (RefEgo reports a **~40-point STIoU gap**). A hand-annotated archive of 400–1,100 h is not a realistic solo project; this is the empirical argument for pipeline (1)/(4) above. https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07058 · https://ego4d-data.org/
- **"Can ChatGPT Learn My Life From a Week of First-Person Video?"** (Harris, CMU, 2025) is the closest single-person analogue: 54 h of head-cam over one week, converted to **hierarchical minute → hour → day summaries**, then used to finetune GPT-4o / GPT-4o-mini. It recovered city, occupation, handedness, and pet ownership. **Failure mode named explicitly: hallucinating names for people in the footage.** The transferable design is the *hierarchical temporal summary* as the finetuning substrate — you never feed the model video, you feed it a compressed textual timeline. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03857
- **KeeganGPT** (same line of work) shows lightweight next-token finetuning on **auto-generated temporally-structured summaries** teaches an LLM facts about a person's life on minimal compute.
- **VideoRAG** (Findings of ACL 2025) is the retrieval-first alternative to finetuning: dynamic video retrieval, informative frame subset selection, and **auto-generated transcripts when subtitles are absent** — because LVLM context cannot hold all frames of a long video. Code: https://github.com/starsuzi/VideoRAG · https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1096/
- **LifelongMemory** — concise activity descriptions over long-form egocentric video + zero-shot LLM reasoning for NL QA/retrieval. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05269
- **EgoStream** (2026) — 2,250 questions expanded to 8,528 recall-conditioned evals over egocentric streams up to **45.3 h**, across 7 cognitive memory dimensions. The relevant framing: episodic-memory retrieval *"uniquely emphasizes personalization, with most queries explicitly referencing personal objects"* and *"history memory spanning months."* https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31557
- **Rethinking RAG in Long Videos: What to Retrieve and How to Use It?** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13141 · **AdaVideoRAG** https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13589 · **MA-LMM** (memory-augmented long-video LMM)
- Applied pattern from the systems side: sample at **0.2 fps (1 frame / 5 s)**, CLIP-embed, store in a vector DB (Qdrant/LanceDB), use **qHNSW for disk-resident approximate search** so billions of vectors don't need TB of RAM. Semantic chunking on scene changes beats fixed-interval chunking. https://kx.com/blog/revolutionizing-video-search-with-multimodal-ai/ · https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/multimodal-rag-for-advanced-video-processing-with-llamaindex-lancedb-33be4804822e
- **Commercial baseline to beat.** Medal.tv/Eklipse/Spikes do post-hoc audio+activity heuristics; **Outplayed/Overwolf hooks the game event API directly**, which is more reliable than post-hoc vision. NVIDIA Highlights needs per-game developer integration. The consistently reported weakness: *"the AI decides what is clip-worthy, which means it misses context-dependent moments that only you would recognize."* That personalization gap — a model trained on *your* archive and *your* saved clips — is the only defensible wedge against these tools. https://blog.eklipse.gg/tools/ai-clip-maker-gaming.html · https://medal.tv/compare/medal-vs-outplayed

## WHAT-THE-LITERATURE-SAYS-IS-HARD

1. **Skill/rank prediction from gameplay video does not generalize across players.** The only paper that tested it honestly (user-held-out split) found every video model at or below the **F1 0.500** majority baseline, with the "good" 0.850 result caused by user identity leaking across splits. Authors: *"our proposed models is prone to identifying users instead of learning meaningful representations."* If Sohum builds a skill model on his own archive alone, he has **n = 1 player** — the exact regime this failure mode punishes hardest. Anything that looks like "my aim improved" must be validated against an independent signal (rank history, Aim Lab telemetry), never against the model's own confidence. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12236

2. **HUD OCR on Valorant specifically is a known dead end.** Valoscribe's authors abandoned OCR: Valorant's **custom font defeats OCR models**, and OCR is **too slow at 4 FPS** for near-realtime. VideoGameQA-Bench (4,786 questions, 9 tasks) finds VLMs *"perform poorly at detecting fine details required for accurate scene understanding and parsing complex UI elements."* AMD's whole architectural argument is that YOLO+OCR needs *"extensive per game engineering"* while X-CLIP generalizes across a genre without it. https://github.com/SphinxNumberNine/valoscribe · https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15952

3. **Video LLMs are much worse at temporal reasoning than headline benchmark numbers suggest.** Apple's VBenchComp finds many benchmark questions are answerable **without watching the video**, and that scores are **largely unchanged under temporal frame shuffling**. TempCompass reaches the same conclusion via reversal/concatenation manipulations. Practical consequence: a VLM that scores well on "was this a good play?" may be reading a single frame's HUD, not the play. Ablate with shuffled and single-frame inputs before trusting any VLM labeler. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14321 · https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01167v2

4. **Frontier-vs-open gap on esports video is enormous and unresolved.** EgoEsportsQA: **GPT-5 71.58%** vs Qwen3-VL-8B 50.26%, InternVL-3.5-8B 37.82%, LLaVA-OneVision 33.52% — and even GPT-5 is at 71.58%, not 95%. Local open-weight VLMs on a DGX Spark are **not** currently good enough to serve as the Valorant labeling oracle. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12320

5. **Temporal localization precision is the unsolved part, not classification.** SoccerNet's spotting Average-mAP is **49.7% at tolerances of 5–60 s** while 1-minute segment *classification* is 67.8% mAP. On QVHighlights, 2026 SOTA highlight-detection mAP is **~44.43** (CVA) with HIT@1 ~65.69 — far below the 90%+ clip-level classification numbers. Domain shift makes this worse: TAL methods assume i.i.d. train/test and *"untrimmed video data for TAL inherently contains various gaps — visual, annotation, and montage"*. **Deciding a clip contains a kill is easy (>93%); deciding exactly where the highlight starts and ends is not.** https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04527 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24934 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13311

6. **Audio-only over-triggers.** Ringer & Nicolaou: the audio stream *"produced considerably more detected highlights than other modalities, likely due to the impact of in-game audio (e.g. gunfire)."* PANNs notes gunshots last only **hundreds of milliseconds**, and the general SED literature warns that tuning down false alarms from similar sounds risks missing real events, with rare high-stakes sounds having the least training data. Audio is a **recall** instrument; precision must come from elsewhere. https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09715 · https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10211

7. **Small annotated sets bite the rarer classes hardest.** In AMD's own INT8 results, the two most Valorant-relevant *ability* classes degrade most: **Power Use 83.6%**, **Reload 86.2%** vs Bomb Planted 99.2% / Death 96.2%. Overwatch 2 — the most ability-dense title — collapses from 89.7% → **76.9%** under quantization while Valorant is stable at 95.2%. Ability/ultimate detection is materially harder than kill detection. https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07721v1

8. **The legal risk is real and recent.** Reka's CS2-10k — 10,000+ hours, 63.5 TB, 18,159 downloads, CC BY-NC 4.0, built from *public HLTV pro demos* — was **taken down by a Valve notice** between 2026-06-24 and 2026-08-13. EgoCS-400K, same provenance, still says "Data Coming soon." Sohum's own ShadowPlay recordings of his own matches are a different posture from redistributing rendered pro demos, but **publishing a derived Valorant video dataset is a live legal question, not a settled one.** Riot's API policy separately gates production keys and, since 2025-03-13, bans enemy-ultimate tracking in third-party apps. https://huggingface.co/datasets/RekaAI/CS2-10k · https://www.riotgames.com/en/DevRel/valorant-api-launch

9. **Manual annotation at this scale is not a solo project.** Ego4D densely annotated only 48–1,000 h of 3,670 h with institutional funding; one HanDyVQA-style annotation effort on top of Ego4D cost **~USD 22,000** outsourced. Any plan that begins with "I'll label a few thousand clips by hand" should be checked against Yahoo's actual numbers: one expert, iterative model-assisted correction, four rounds per game, to cover 300 h. https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07058

## Verified hard facts

- AMD published 'Gameplay Highlights Generation' (Edithal, Zhang, Blank, Junejo, arXiv:2505.07721, May 2025) covering exactly this problem, and Valorant is one of its five games alongside CS:GO, PUBG, Overwatch 2 and Fortnite.
- That paper finetunes X-CLIP-B/16 (Kinetics-400 pretrained, K600 finetuned) on 32 RGB frames at 224x224 and reports 94.3% overall accuracy, 0.93 F-score, 0.99 AUC, with Valorant at 95.0% (95.2% after INT8 quantization).
- Its INT8 ONNX model is 192 MB (down from 768 MB), runs at ~0.15 seconds per 1-second gameplay clip, uses 800 MB RAM and 1,500 MB VRAM, and costs 13 FPS on an 80 FPS Fortnite baseline.
- Its training set was 4,408 human-annotated events plus 2,745 algorithmically sampled background instances (7,153 total) over 110 GB of video; the dataset was NOT released.
- Its per-event INT8 accuracies are Bomb Planted 99.2%, Death 96.2%, Kill 93.6%, Grenade Throw 93.1%, Background 94.2%, Knocked Down 90.0%, Reload 86.2%, Power Use 83.6% - ability-type events are the weakest.
- AMD's conclusion states verbatim: 'In our work we did not use the audio modality of gameplay videos' and notes 'in CS:GO a unique sound is played whenever a kill event occurs', naming audio integration as future work.
- Reka's RekaAI/CS2-10k (600,000+ player-round videos, 10,000+ hours, 720p/48fps, per-frame keyboard/mouse/3D-position annotations, 63.48 TB, 18,159 downloads, CC BY-NC 4.0) was TAKEN DOWN; its README reads 'This dataset is no longer available due to a takedown notice from Valve.' Created 2026-06-24, gutted 2026-08-13.
- EgoCS-400K (arXiv:2606.18180), built from the same HLTV pro-demo provenance and claiming 400K+ videos / 10,000+ hours, is not downloadable - its site says 'Data Coming soon.'
- TeaPearce/CounterStrike_Deathmatch is the only large public FPS gameplay-video dataset actually hosted and permissively licensed: ~4M frames, 767 GB, MIT license, backing Pearce & Zhu's IEEE CoG 2022 Best Paper (arXiv:2104.04258).
- There is no public Valorant gameplay-VIDEO dataset at any useful scale: a full Hugging Face API search returns only still-image object detection sets (largest: keremberke/valorant-object-detection, 9,898 images, CC BY 4.0), agent voicelines, and text.
- BattleSound (Sensors 2023, PUBG, 6.7 hours, 47,046 samples at 0.5s / 16 kHz) reports 96.02% +/- 0.08 weapon sound event detection accuracy with a 2D CNN on mel-spectrograms; dataset openly available at sites.google.com/view/battlesound.
- 'Enemy Spotted' (Park et al., IEEE CoG 2022, arXiv:2210.05917) classifies 37 gun types from in-game PUBG audio at 93.60% test accuracy / 0.9290 F1 with a CNN-Transformer; Bi-LSTM (0.6068) and plain Transformer (0.5918) fail, showing convolutional spectrogram features are load-bearing.
- Della Santa & Lalli (arXiv:2501.16100, 2025) found audio BEAT video on football highlight detection: audio accuracy 0.8924 / F1 0.8923 vs video accuracy 0.8300 / F1 0.8301, using fewer training chunks.
- AVSlowFast (Xiao et al., FAIR, arXiv:2001.08740) states verbatim that 'the [Audio] pathway typically only requires 10% to 20% of the overall [computation]', and adding audio gave +2.9/+4.3/+2.3 top-1 on EPIC-Kitchens verbs/nouns/actions and 75.6 -> 77.0 on Kinetics R50.
- Zhang & Wang (arXiv:2307.12236) showed CS:GO rank prediction from video FAILS on a user-held-out split: every video model scored at or below the 0.500 majority-class F1 baseline (Multiview 0.470, Top View 0.490, LR 0.487), while the leaky video-based split reached 0.850 - the authors state the model 'is prone to identifying users instead of learning meaningful representations.'
- EgoEsportsQA (arXiv:2604.12320, 2026) covers CS2, Valorant and Overwatch 2 with 364 clips / 1,745 QA pairs / ~7.5 hours, and finds GPT-5 at 71.58% vs open 8B models at 33-50% (Qwen3-VL-8B 50.26%, InternVL-3.5-8B 37.82%, LLaVA-OneVision 33.52%).
- Unsupervised highlight detection is nearly as good as supervised: Islam/Paul/Rochan (arXiv:2407.13933) reach 68.30 mAP on YouTube Highlights with zero labels versus 70.18 for the same architecture trained supervised, using K-means pseudo-categories over audio-visual features.
- Weak supervision by aligning raw footage against already-edited highlight reels is a proven label-free technique: SVHighlights (KDD 2026, arXiv:2606.06926) generated labels for 640.18 hours / 320 two-hour videos this way, and the Honor of Kings paper (arXiv:1910.06189) used pairwise ranking between edited clips and long VODs.
- Yahoo's production esports highlight system (Song, arXiv:1611.08780) labeled 300 hours with ONE expert via iterative model-assisted correction (~10h from scratch, then 20h, 30h, four rounds per game), achieving AP 83.18 on HotS at 18 FPS on a single CPU with a cascaded scene-gate + highlight-classifier design.
- Temporal localization, not classification, is the unsolved part: SoccerNet spotting Average-mAP is 49.7% at 5-60s tolerance vs 67.8% mAP for 1-minute segment classification, and 2026 QVHighlights SOTA highlight-detection mAP is ~44.43.
- Apple's VBenchComp (arXiv:2505.14321, NeurIPS 2025 workshop) found video LLM benchmark scores are largely unchanged when frames are temporally shuffled and many questions are answerable without watching the video at all.
- Aim skill improves slowly and is only measurable at scale: Listman et al. 2021 (7,174 players, 62,170 game-days, 682,564 Gridshot runs) found hit rate moved 86.10% -> 89.60% over 60 days (~0.03%/day) and that 90% of the learning benefit comes from 30 min/day.
- The Flicking Skill Assessment (Donovan et al., Front. Hum. Neurosci. 2022, 32 pro players including Valorant) requires 120 Hz MOUSE TRACES, not video, and its core finding is that a single metric cannot define skill - the whole speed-accuracy tradeoff curve must be fit.
- Valorant HUD OCR is a documented dead end: the Valoscribe project reports Valorant's custom font defeats OCR models and OCR at 4 FPS is too slow for near-realtime, and AMD argues YOLO+OCR needs 'extensive per game engineering'.
- Ego4D collected 3,670 narrated hours but densely annotated only 48-1,000 hours per benchmark, demonstrating that hand-annotating a 400-1,100 hour personal archive is not a realistic solo project.
- Riot Games gates VALORANT API production keys per-application and, since 2025-03-13, prohibits enemy-ultimate-ability tracking in all third-party applications.

## Open questions

- The actual duration of the 10TB archive is UNVERIFIED. My 400-1,100 hour estimate assumes 20-50 Mbps ShadowPlay mp4; at 4K60 (~130 Mbps) it would be closer to 170 hours. Run ffprobe over a random sample of files to get real bitrate, resolution, fps and audio codec/channel layout - this single number changes the compute budget by 6x and decides whether a full X-CLIP sweep (60-165 GPU-hours at AMD's 0.15s/1s rate) is trivial or expensive.
- Whether Sohum has ANY manually-saved, trimmed, or posted clips derived from the raw DVR footage. This is the highest-value unknown: SVHighlights and the Honor of Kings paper both show that pairing raw footage against already-edited highlights generates labels for hundreds of hours at zero annotation cost. If he has even a few hundred hand-picked clips, the labeling problem is largely solved. If not, he falls back to unsupervised recurrence (68.30 mAP ceiling) or frontier-VLM labeling.
- Whether his clips have separate microphone/party-voice audio tracks or only mixed game audio. ShadowPlay can record system + mic on separate tracks. Separate tracks would make teammate-reaction detection (the Ringer & Nicolaou 'social signal') tractable; a single mixed track makes it much harder, and mixed-in Discord voice raises a consent question if anything is ever published.
- Whether the AMD authors will share the 7,153-instance gameplay event dataset on request. The paper makes no availability statement and I found no public release. An email to Vignesh Edithal / Imran Junejo at AMD is cheap and would eliminate the single largest bootstrapping cost, since their annotation schema (7 event classes + algorithmic background sampling) is already validated on Valorant at 95%.
